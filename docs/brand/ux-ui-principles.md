# UX/UI Principles

Intentionally's interface should feel authentic, calm, safe, premium, and intentional.

Every screen should help users feel more in control, not more overwhelmed.

## Design Inspiration

Intentionally should learn from strong product references without copying them directly.

Apple represents polished, intuitive, spacious, premium simplicity.

Hinge represents relationship-focused, prompt-led, human, and intentional dating flows.

Headspace represents calm, reassuring, emotionally safe, and gentle product design.

Intentionally should combine Apple's clarity and polish, Hinge's relationship-first flow, and Headspace's calm emotional tone into its own distinct product experience.

## 1. Design Purpose

Design exists to help users make clearer, safer, more intentional relationship decisions.

The interface should reduce ambiguity, support trust, and guide users toward meaningful progress without creating pressure.

## 2. Emotional Experience

The product should feel calm, respectful, and emotionally safe.

Users should feel that the app understands the sensitivity of dating and protects them from unnecessary urgency, exposure, and performance pressure.

The experience should create confidence without becoming cold or clinical.

## Healthy Dopamine

Intentionally should create moments of delight, anticipation, and reward only when they support meaningful connection and user wellbeing.

Reward meaningful progress, not compulsive usage.

Celebrate mutual effort, not popularity.

Use subtle delight rather than addictive loops.

Make successful moments feel warm, memorable, and emotionally safe.

Avoid casino-style mechanics, streak pressure, fake urgency, infinite reward loops, or shame-based notifications.

Examples:

- A soft glow when two users mutually choose to continue.
- A warm success state after a Vibe Check is completed.
- Gentle celebration when a date plan is created.
- Reassuring progress feedback during onboarding.
- Calm anticipation before a scheduled Vibe Check.

## 3. Visual Principles

The visual system should use warm, grounded, human colours: cream, sage, warm brown, and soft neutrals.

Visual weight should be quiet and deliberate. Avoid loud gradients, aggressive contrast, visual clutter, and decorative elements that distract from trust or clarity.

Premium should mean composed, restrained, and thoughtful, not flashy.

## 4. Layout Principles

Layouts should prioritise clarity, breathing room, and obvious next steps.

Important actions should be easy to find, but not visually coercive. Secondary actions should be available without competing with the main task.

Screens should avoid horizontal scrolling, cramped spacing, and stacked decisions that create cognitive overload.

## 5. Component Principles

Components should be small, reusable, accessible, and consistent with the existing design system.

Buttons, cards, forms, modals, sheets, and navigation should use familiar patterns and clear states. Component styling should support trust and legibility before novelty.

Primary actions should represent meaningful progress. Destructive, irreversible, or emotionally sensitive actions should be explicit and calm.

## 6. Motion and Animation Principles

Motion should clarify state changes, not entertain at the user's expense.

Animations should be subtle, fast, and respectful of reduced-motion preferences. They should never delay a user's ability to act or understand what happened.

Use motion to support orientation, feedback, and continuity.

## 7. Copy and Microcopy Principles

Copy should be clear, gentle, and direct.

Microcopy should reduce pressure, explain consequences, and make privacy and safety boundaries obvious.

Avoid hype, manipulation, shame, fear, or language that makes users feel behind, rejected, or disposable.

## 8. Accessibility Principles

Accessibility is part of product quality.

Text should be legible, contrast should be sufficient, focus states should be visible, and interactive targets should be large enough to tap comfortably.

The product should support keyboard navigation, screen readers, reduced motion, and clear error recovery wherever possible.

## 9. Mobile-First Principles

Intentionally should be designed for mobile use first.

Mobile layouts should account for safe areas, browser chrome, fixed navigation, thumb reach, and small-screen emotional load.

Critical actions should not be hidden, overlapped, or placed where fixed controls make them hard to use.

## 10. UX Anti-Patterns Intentionally Should Avoid

Do not optimise for addictive swiping, endless browsing, or urgency loops.

Do not use dark patterns, forced scarcity, shame, streaks, or manipulative notifications.

Do not blur privacy, safety, or consent boundaries.

Do not create decision overload with too many competing calls to action.

Do not make users feel punished for moving slowly, pausing, passing, or choosing not to continue.

Do not make the product feel like a dashboard, marketplace, or game when the user is navigating emotionally meaningful decisions.
